##About & research

Hendrik Erz is a computational social scientist at the Institute for Analytical Sociology (IAS), Linkoping University, on the Campus Norrkoping in Sweden. He defended his PhD in analytical sociology in 2025. His work sits at the meeting point of political sociology and computational social science: he reads political texts at scale to understand how policy is made and how political regimes change.

Method and question are tightly bound in his research. He uses computational text analysis and natural language processing, alongside network science, to study legislative debates and other political language. He is also drawn to the science of science, the study of how research itself is produced, and to the ethics of doing computational and AI research responsibly.

###The dissertation

His 2025 dissertation, On the Record: Understanding a Century of Congressional Lawmaking Through Speech and Vote Behavior, asks what a hundred years of US Congressional debate and voting can tell us about how laws are actually made. By treating speeches and votes together as a long historical record, it traces the mechanisms of lawmaking across generations rather than across a single session.

> Political texts are not just records of decisions. Read carefully and at scale, they are evidence of the processes that produced those decisions.

###Current projects

  • Discursive polarization in German and Swedish parliamentary debates, tracing how political language pulls apart over time.
  • Ethical behaviour in computer-science security research, examining the norms and incentives that shape how researchers handle vulnerabilities.
  • Swedish folk high schools (folkhogskolan), studied as institutional responses to social change.

##Open-source work

Hendrik is a long-time advocate for open-source software, and much of what he builds is for the people who do research and writing for a living, including himself.

★ ~13,000 stars on GitHub

Zettlr

Zettlr is an open-source, Markdown-based publication workbench for researchers and writers. Hendrik created it in 2017 and continues to maintain it. It brings drafting, citation, and note-taking into one calm writing environment, the same paper-like, distraction-light feeling this page borrows.

TypeScript Electron Markdown open source

He has also built Citr, a citation-parsing library, and Academics on Mastodon, a directory that helps scholars find one another. His code lives on GitHub under the handle nathanlesage.

##Community & background

Hendrik grew up and studied in Germany. He holds a BA in History, with a minor in Political Science, and an MA in Sociology, both from the University of Bonn, before completing his PhD at Linkoping University. He works in German and English.

He gives a good deal of time to the research community. He is a co-organizer of the Swedish Interdisciplinary Research School in Computational Social Science (SIRCSS), and has helped organise conferences including the New Security Paradigms Workshop 2024, IC2S2 2025, and EUSN 2026. He is also an editor of Soziologiemagazin.
