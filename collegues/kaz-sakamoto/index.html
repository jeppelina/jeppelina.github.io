// 01 Research

Measuring poverty from above

Kaz works in computational social science, at the point where causal inference, machine learning and computer vision, and Earth observation meet. His central question is practical: how can satellite imagery be turned into credible evidence about poverty and development?

Reliable data on living standards is scarce in many parts of the world, while satellites image the planet continuously. Machine learning can read structure out of those images, such as settlement patterns, land cover, or built density. The harder step is making that reading causal: moving from what an image shows to what would change if a policy or intervention were applied.

One idea his review examines closely is image-based deconfounding. Programmes are rarely placed at random: the areas that receive them tend to differ from the areas that do not, in terrain, infrastructure, settlement boundaries, or how built-up they are. A satellite image captures many of those differences even when no survey recorded them. Adjusting for what the image sees can bring treated and untreated areas into balance, so the two can be compared as if the programme had been spread evenly.

// 03 Selected work

Scoping review

Lead author · arXiv:2406.02584 Forthcoming book chapter

A Scoping Review of Earth Observation and Machine Learning for Causal Inference: Implications for the Geography of Poverty

Kazuki Sakamoto, Connor T. Jerzak, Adel Daoud

The review maps how Earth observation data and machine learning can be combined with causal analysis, and what that means for studying poverty across places. It synthesises five distinct approaches for connecting satellite imagery to causal questions.

01
Outcome imputation
Using imagery to estimate outcomes that are otherwise unmeasured.
02
Image-based deconfounding
Using imagery to adjust for confounders not captured in survey data.
03
Treatment-effect heterogeneity
Using imagery to study how effects vary across places.
04
Transportability
Carrying causal estimates from one setting to another.
05
Image-informed causal discovery
Using imagery to inform the structure of causal relationships.

The review is forthcoming as a chapter in the book Geography of Poverty (Edward Elgar Publishing).
