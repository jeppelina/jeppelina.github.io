No. 01 The Profile

An economist reading the Swedish school system

How families choose schools, how the state holds them to account, and how struggling schools recover.

Axel Norgren is a PhD student in economics at Linköping University, based at the Institute for Analytical Sociology on the Norrköping campus. His work sits in the economics of education, and it takes the Swedish school system as both its subject and its laboratory: a setting where school choice is widespread, where an inspectorate audits quality, and where the consequences of both reach well beyond the classroom.

The questions he pursues are practical ones. When parents are free to pick a school, what are they actually choosing, and what does the sum of those choices do to segregation between schools and to how effective schools become? When the state inspects and holds schools accountable, does that scrutiny raise quality, or simply move it around? And when a school is failing, what does a credible recovery look like?

Norgren also studies the loop that ties housing to schooling. A family's choice of where to live shapes the schools within easy reach, and the schools within reach shape where families want to live. Treating residential choice and school choice as one connected decision, rather than two separate ones, changes how the resulting patterns of sorting should be read.

He holds an MSc in Economics from Linköping University and works across the boundary between economics and analytical sociology, publishing in both Swedish and English.

No. 02 Lines of Inquiry

Research focus

Four threads running through the economics of education in Sweden.

I.

School choice

Examining what families weigh when they select a school, and how a system built on choice distributes pupils and resources across schools.

II.

Segregation & effectiveness

Tracing how the aggregate of individual choices feeds into segregation between schools and into measured differences in school effectiveness.

III.

Accountability

Assessing accountability systems and the role of the Swedish Schools Inspectorate in supervising and lifting the quality of schooling.

IV.

School turnaround

Studying how underperforming schools recover, and why turnaround tends to follow several pathways rather than a single fix.
