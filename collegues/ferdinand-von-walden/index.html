I The Proclamation

Let it be known throughout the land

A formal introduction to his most distinguished personage.

Hear ye, hear ye. Let the record show that Ferdinand von Walden — holder of both a medical degree and a doctorate from Karolinska Institutet, because apparently one was not enough — has devoted his considerable talents to the noble pursuit of understanding why muscles do what muscles do. Where lesser mortals see a bicep, von Walden sees an entire universe of ribosomes assembling proteins, and he will not rest until every last one of those ribosomes has been catalogued, interrogated, and published about.

By day, he is a resident in pediatrics at Astrid Lindgren Children's Hospital, tending to small humans with the patience of a saint. By night (and also by day, and also on weekends), he leads a research team of eight brave souls investigating skeletal muscle physiology, ribosome biogenesis, and the systemic effects of exercise. One might say he has two full-time jobs, but that would imply he occasionally sleeps.

His academic trajectory is the sort that makes other researchers quietly close their laptops. MD in 2009. PhD in 2015, with a thesis on — and this is real — Ribosome Biogenesis During Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy. Docent in 2025. Somewhere in there he found time to amass over sixty publications, publish in Cell, and maintain what we can only assume is a punishing gym routine. For research purposes, of course.

His name, von Walden, evokes a Bavarian count surveying his estates from horseback. In reality, he surveys Western blots from a lab bench in Solna, which is basically the same thing.

II Domains of Dominion

The territories under his scientific reign

Each conquered through years of pipetting, publishing, and presumably sighing.

🧬
The Kingdom of Ribosome Biogenesis
his core obsession since 2012

Ribosomes: the tiny molecular machines that build proteins. Ferdinand studies how muscles make more of them when you exercise. He has written more words about ribosomal RNA than most people have written about anything, ever. His doctoral thesis was literally called “Ribosome Biogenesis During Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy.” At parties, this is what he talks about. At this party, we are talking about it too. You are welcome.

💪
The Duchy of Skeletal Muscle
molecular muscle physiology in health & disease

Why do muscles grow? Why do they waste away? What does MYC have to do with anything? Ferdinand's team showed that 24 hours of molecular data after resistance exercise implicate the MYC oncogene as a key driver of muscle growth — and that overexpressing MYC alone is sufficient for hypertrophy. Published in EMBO Reports. Casual.

🚴
The Province of Pediatric Exercise
making children run, for science

Ferdinand's team developed and validated the 6-Minute Frame Running Test for individuals with cerebral palsy, published the protocol in JoVE (yes, a video journal — the man has range), and demonstrated that RaceRunning training promotes skeletal muscle hypertrophy in young individuals with CP. He is, in the most literal sense, helping kids build muscle.

🧪
The Barony of Epigenetics
DNA methylation & muscle memory

Muscles remember. Ferdinand has published on myonuclear DNA methylation and the concept of epigenetic “muscle memory” — the idea that your muscles retain a molecular record of previous training. Your biceps are keeping a diary, and Ferdinand is reading it.

🩺
The Principality of Clinical Pediatrics
actual medical doctor, astrid lindgren hospital

Lest we forget: between all the pipetting and publishing, Ferdinand is also an actual doctor who sees actual patients at one of Sweden's most beloved children's hospitals, named after the woman who wrote Pippi Longstocking. There is no known connection between his research and Pippi's superhuman strength, but we are not ruling it out.

🌍
The Colony of International Collaboration
UAB, Arkansas, Amsterdam, Copenhagen, & beyond

Ferdinand's publication list reads like a United Nations roll call. Collaborators from the University of Arkansas, UAB, Amsterdam, Copenhagen, and beyond. He gave a seminar at UAB in October 2025 on “Skeletal Muscle Biology in Health and Disease.” Alabama was never the same.

IV The Sacred Texts

Selected works from the von Walden canon

Ranked not by impact factor, but by how impressive they sound at dinner.

Magnum Opus Cell · 2025

Systems-level immunomonitoring in children with solid tumors to enable precision medicine

Chen Q, Zhao B, Tan Z, … von Walden F, … Brodin P — Cell, 2025; 188(5): 1425–1440

Published in Cell. The journal so prestigious that merely appearing in its table of contents causes your h-index to visibly twitch. Ferdinand's contribution to pediatric precision medicine at its finest.

The MYC Manifesto EMBO Reports · 2024

The 24-hour molecular landscape after exercise in humans reveals MYC is sufficient for muscle growth

Edman S, Jones RG III, Jannig PR, … von Walden F — EMBO Reports, 2024; 25(12): 5810–5837

The paper that proved MYC alone is sufficient for muscle hypertrophy. Twenty-seven pages of molecular evidence that your muscles have an oncogene-powered growth button. Ferdinand: “We found it.” Everyone else: “Please stop pressing it.”

The Origin Story Am J Physiol · 2012

Mechanical loading induces the expression of a Pol I regulon at the onset of skeletal muscle hypertrophy

von Walden F, Casagrande V, Farrants A-KO, Nader GA — Am J Physiol Cell Physiol, 2012; 302(10): C1523–C1530

The paper that started it all. Ferdinand proved that lifting heavy things activates RNA Polymerase I and kicks off ribosome production. In other words: he was the first to explain what happens inside your cells when you pick up a dumbbell. The scientific equivalent of an origin story.

Does Time Tick Faster? FASEB J · 2026

Does Time Tick Faster in Cerebral Palsy? Accelerated Aging as a Framework for Skeletal Muscle Dysfunction

Horwath O, Edman S, Dayanidhi S, Englund D, Peterson MD, von Walden F — FASEB Journal, 2026; 40(6): e71653

A title so evocative it could be a novel. Ferdinand's team proposes that skeletal muscle in cerebral palsy ages faster than expected, reframing the entire field. Bold, compassionate, and beautifully written for a research paper.

VI Orders & Honours

Decorations of the realm

A selection of achievements, real and editorially enhanced.

  • 🎓

    Docent of Clinical Muscle Physiology

    Karolinska Institutet · 2025

    Awarded the title of Docent — the Swedish academic rank that roughly translates to “we formally acknowledge you know an unreasonable amount about this subject.” Ferdinand's subject: clinical muscle physiology. His students: warned.

  • 📖

    Published in Cell

    2025 · Impact Factor: ~65

    Publishing in Cell is the academic equivalent of getting a five-star Michelin review. Most researchers dream about it. Ferdinand did it while also being a practicing pediatrician. Naturally.

  • 🏃

    Pioneer of Frame Running Science

    Ongoing since ~2018

    Frame running — a sport designed for people with balance impairments — now has a validated exercise test, physiological benchmarks, and training evidence, largely because Ferdinand decided it should. He and his team have published at least seven papers on the topic. Frame running has a champion, and it is him.

  • 🧬

    The MYC Discovery

    2024

    Demonstrated that a single gene (MYC) is sufficient to drive muscle growth. The paper spawned an editorial, a follow-up, and presumably several confused gym-goers Googling “how to activate MYC.”

  • ⚔️

    Dual Wielder of Medicine and Science

    Since 2009

    Simultaneously maintains a clinical residency in pediatrics and an active research lab. This is the academic equivalent of playing two instruments in the same orchestra at the same time, while also conducting.
