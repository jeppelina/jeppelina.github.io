About the research

Tracing how behaviour and health inequality travel through networks

My research treats health behaviour as something that moves between people. Rather than studying individuals in isolation, I look at the social ties that connect them, and ask how habits, norms and inequalities in health are passed along those connections.

A central strand of this work examines how nicotine products, including cigarettes, e-cigarettes and snus, diffuse among Swedish adolescents, both online and offline. Social network analysis makes it possible to see which connections carry influence, and how an adopted behaviour can ripple outward from a small starting point to reshape a wider group.

Alongside the work on nicotine use, I also study social influence on fertility: how decisions and norms around having children are shaped by the people around us.

Current project

Studying the spread of nicotine products in teenage networks

Tackling the spread of cigarettes and alternative nicotine products in social networks

This project follows how cigarettes, e-cigarettes and snus move through the social networks of Swedish teenagers. It collects social network data from around 1,000 adolescents and links it to Swedish administrative registers, combining a close view of peer connections with population-level health and background information.
